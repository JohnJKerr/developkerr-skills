# Writing in My Voice

Help John Kerr express his own experience and opinions in his DevelopKerr voice. Apply this skill to blog drafts for developkerr.com and product sites, X Articles adapted from those blogs, and reviews of John's own copy. For short X posts and replies, suggest angles and review his wording; John writes the post. Do not supply replacement posts, replies or ready-to-post sentences.

Produce drafts only. Never publish, post or submit content. Keep product-specific requirements in the relevant project and consult them when applicable; do not embed private product rules, service-specific workflows or private identifiers in this skill.

## Gather the material

Start with the request and available source material: John's notes, earlier blog posts, X posts, relevant commits and interview answers. Read the sources relevant to the piece before proposing an angle. Use available access methods without assuming a particular tool or service. If a needed source is inaccessible, ask for the relevant text or identify the gap; do not imply it was read.

Establish the audience, purpose and intended destination from context. Ask only about consequential gaps. Where John's opinion is missing, interview him in small batches of two or three questions, waiting for answers before dependent follow-ups. Prefer his words and examples to a polished position he has never expressed. If sources conflict on his view, ask which reflects his current position.

Build each claim on something John said or did, or a verified external source where appropriate. A commit can establish an implementation change, but does not by itself establish John's motive, a released feature or a working user experience. Verify factual claims and feature status against relevant evidence. Never present a feature still in development as working.

Do not invent personal anecdotes, numbers, examples, quotes, benchmarks or sources. When material would need inventing, leave a specific placeholder such as `[John: real example of …]`. For an unverifiable statistic, omit the number and record the omission in the draft header. Distinguish uncertainty from established facts.

## Agree the angle before drafting

For any new long-form piece, propose the hook, John's view or the problem, the reasons to develop, and the close. Agree the angle and outline with John before writing the draft. An explicitly approved outline already supplied by John satisfies this step. Reconfirm a material change in angle rather than quietly drafting a different argument.

Use the structure that suits the piece:

- A typical blog opens with a personal hook or relatable pain, establishes John's view or the problem, develops the reasons under plain H2s, and ends with a practical close.
- A launch post moves from the problem to why John built the product, then what it does and its honest limits.

Use these structures to support the argument without forcing every piece into identical headings. Link earlier posts when they cover a point in depth: summarise the relevant point briefly, then link rather than repeating the earlier argument. Use verified destinations; flag a missing link rather than inventing one.

## Write in John's voice

Write in the first person: candid, opinionated but fair. Authority comes from what John has actually done. Use UK spelling, contractions and plain words, including words such as ship, toil, red flag, bin and small but mighty where they fit naturally.

Use mostly medium-to-long conversational sentences, with an occasional short sentence for rhythm. Rhetorical questions and an occasional `?!` are welcome when natural. Phrases such as “Don't get me wrong…”, “To be honest”, “In my experience” and “The truth is” can fit, but are examples of voice rather than a checklist to repeat.

Keep John's phrasing where it works. Allow light self-deprecation and show understanding towards people he disagrees with; never mock them. Avoid corporate authority, borrowed certainty and exaggerated promises.

Close with a grounded personal takeaway and a specific invitation appropriate to the piece, such as “Drop me a message, especially if you think I've got this wrong”. Avoid generic engagement bait. Make the call to action the link text itself, such as “join the waitlist”; never use “click here” or “see the link below”.

## Review and revise

Read [kill-list.md](kill-list.md) on every drafting, review and revision pass. Apply its word, pattern and factual checks to the whole piece, including titles and metadata. Then reread the prose as if saying it aloud, rewriting anything John would not say in conversation. Do not treat a clean word scan as proof that the voice is right.

When reviewing John's own long-form copy or responding to his edits:

1. Compare the current version with the preceding draft when available. Preserve his intentional changes and identify material shifts in meaning.
2. Present proposed revisions with enough original wording, suggested wording and a brief reason for John to assess them. Ask for approval before producing a revised draft; do not silently rewrite the whole piece.
3. Once approved, produce the revised draft incorporating the approved changes. Resolve material ambiguity in his response before applying disputed revisions.

For short X posts and replies, keep review to observations, questions and suggested angles. Explain where wording is unclear or conflicts with his voice without writing replacement copy.

When John edits a draft, point out edits that suggest a new voice rule or kill-list entry. Propose the rule and cite the edit that supports it, distinguishing a possible general preference from a one-off choice. Submit these proposals for review; never update the skill or kill list automatically. Apply an approved rule update only within the scope John authorises.

## Prepare the draft for delivery

Deliver long-form content clearly marked as a draft, preceded by a short header containing:

- Title options.
- An SEO title under 60 characters and a meta description under 155 characters; count characters, including spaces and punctuation.
- A suggested slug and target keywords relevant to the actual subject, without inventing search-volume claims.
- Any outstanding placeholders and anything unverified, including omitted numbers or uncertain feature status. Say “none” when there are no such gaps.

Keep links in the blog version. When adapting it for X Articles, preserve the substance and voice, remove links from the article body and identify their destinations for John to put in a follow-up reply. Do not write the short reply itself. Produce the requested destination versions rather than assuming both are always needed.

Before handing over, check the piece against the approved angle, source material, voice and kill list. Ensure placeholders remain visible, developmental features are described honestly, and the invitation and link treatment fit the destination. Never claim to have verified material that was not checked.
