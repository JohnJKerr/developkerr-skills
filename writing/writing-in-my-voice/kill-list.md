# Kill List

Read this file on every drafting, review and revision pass. Check titles, metadata and body copy. Rewrite the underlying habit as well as removing the flagged words. Preserve the accuracy of attributed quotes; flag a conflict rather than silently altering a quotation.

## Words and phrases

- Hype and buzzwords: revolutionise, game-changer, seamless, supercharge, unlock, unleash, leverage (as a verb), robust, cutting-edge, next-level, elevate, harness, empower, effortless, best-in-class, world-class.
- AI tells: delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, testament, “in today's fast-paced world”, “it's worth noting”, “let's dive in”, “here's the thing”, “the result?”, “the best part?”, “you're absolutely right”, “in conclusion”, “at its core”, “a deep dive”.
- Empty intensifiers and hedges stacked together, such as “truly”, “incredibly” or “it could be argued that perhaps”.

## Patterns

- Reflexive “It's not X, it's Y” reversals.
- A rule of three in every sentence or list.
- Headline fragments, “Feature name. Description.” lines, “label: value” lines in prose, and arrows or symbols standing in for words. The requested draft metadata header is structured information, not prose.
- Em dashes. John occasionally uses a spaced hyphen, usually just before a list or punchline; do not substitute one for every removed em dash.
- Coined labels, slogans and analogies standing in for the actual mechanism.
- Sweeping claims about what “people” or “the industry” say without identifying who said it. Make it John's supported experience or cut it.
- Imprecise words where a precise one exists: for example, “three clauses” when that is what is meant, rather than “three words”.
- Recap paragraphs restating what the reader just read.
- Generic engagement bait, such as “What do you think? Let me know in the comments!”
- “Navigate” and “ultimately” as filler or reflex openers, such as “Ultimately, …” at the start of a closing paragraph. They are fine when used literally or to land a genuine conclusion.
- Emojis in blog posts. On X, use one at most, only when it serves the meaning; never decorative, never the thread emoji and never hashtag strings.
- US spelling; use UK spelling.

## Facts

- No invented statistics, quotes, benchmarks or sources. If a number cannot be verified, leave it out and say so in the draft header.
- Never claim a feature works when it is still in development.

## Updating this list

Propose additions based on John's edits, showing the supporting change. Obtain his approval before updating this file; do not turn a single edit into an automatic permanent rule.
